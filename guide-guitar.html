Charting Guide · Guitar Mode

How to chart Guitar Mode.

Guitar Mode builds rhythmic riffs across four fret highways with distinct Strum, No-Strum Tap, Long Hold, Bend, and Special Overdrive notes—scaling smoothly from Easy up to 8-Key Crazy.

01 · Frets & Difficulties

Four highway lanes, one or two hands.

Every Guitar chart sits on 4 highway tracks plus a 5th Special track for overdrive phrases. Higher difficulties unlock two-handed 8-Key patterns.

Pacing the 4 Difficulties

  • Easy: Focus on quarter-note and half-note riffs using mainly No-Strum Taps and gentle holds.
  • Normal: Introduce clear Strum Taps (Shift) on downbeats and simple power-chord holds.
  • Hard: Add eighth-note alternating riffs, Bend holds on long guitar sustains, and deliberate strum-to-tap phrasing.
  • Crazy: Combine fast solo phrasing, 8-Key right-hand alternates, tight Bend sustains, and well-paced Special lanes.

02 · Note Types

Match the note type to how the guitar sounds.

Choosing between Strum, Tap, Long, and Bend is what makes a Guitar chart actually feel like playing the riff.

  1. 01

    Strum vs. No-Strum Tap

    Use Strum notes (require Shift) when the guitarist picks or strums a distinct beat, chord, or accented note. Use No-Strum Taps for hammer-ons, pull-offs, legato solo runs, and fast rolling fills where pressing Shift on every 1/16 note would feel muddy.

  2. 02

    Long Holds

    Use Strum Long or No-Strum Long whenever a chord or lead note rings out across beats. Leave at least a small 1/16 gap before the next note on the same lane so players can clearly release and re-press.

  3. 03

    Bend Sustains

    Bend Long notes ask players to hold the fret and Spacebar (vibrato/whammy feel). Save Bends for expressive held solo notes, chorus rings, and dramatic pitch bends rather than short quarter notes.

  4. 04

    Right-Hand 8-Key Splits

    Don't switch back and forth randomly between Z/X/C/V and M/,/./? on every single beat. Group right-hand notes into clean alternating riffs or highlight dual-guitar harmonies.

03 · Special & Flameout

Build tension, then reward players with Flameout.

The 5th track carries Special sustain sections that charge excitement and reward clean execution.

Charting Special Sections

A Special Section sits on the 5th lane for a clear sustain window. Use Special sections before major chorus drops or climactic guitar solos—never overload the entire song with constant Special bars.

Give Players Gauge Time First

Avoid placing the first Special section right in the opening 4 bars. Let players build combo and raise the gauge past 50% during the intro and first verse so Flameout actually triggers cleanly on the chorus.

Comfort & Sightreading Tips

  • Stepwise Pitch Mapping: Map lower guitar notes toward left frets (Z / X) and higher solo notes toward right frets (C / V).
  • Fair Chords: Stick to comfortable 2-note or 3-note power chords; avoid awkward outer spreads at high speeds unless playing Easy/Slow tempos.
  • Consistent Motifs: If a chorus riff repeats three times, keep the same fret contour so players recognize it immediately.

04 · Final Check

Pre-test Guitar checklist.

Playtest every difficulty you author before sharing.

  • BPM and waveform alignment stay exact through solos and endings.
  • Strum notes highlight picked accents while hammer-on runs use clean No-Strum Taps.
  • Long and Bend holds have a small clean release gap before the next note on the same lane.
  • Special sections appear after enough notes to reach 50%+ Flameout gauge.
  • Easy & Normal difficulties omit tiring 8-Key cross-hand jumps.
  • Every phrase feels musically tied to the real guitar track.
