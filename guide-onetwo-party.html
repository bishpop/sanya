Charting Guide · One-Two Party

How to chart One-Two Party.

One-Two Party is a rhythmic Call-and-Response mode: the NPC demonstrates a short pattern first, and the player mirrors the exact rhythm back on the next turn. Keeping turns synchronized and phrases cleanly bounded is the core rule.

01 · Chart Fields

Four lanes control the entire party flow.

Unlike straight note-falling modes, One-Two Party charts organize every round using four distinct event columns.

1. Notes & 2. Changes

FieldPurpose while Charting
1. NotesEach directional arrow beat in the phrase. Determines both the rhythm and the difficulty level (count of notes in the turn).
2. ChangesMarks the start of a new turn switch between the NPC Demonstration and the Player Response.

3. Dance Breaks & 4. Spaces

FieldPurpose while Charting
3. Dance BreakPlaces group dance interludes between Call-and-Response rounds, plus the Final Pose at the end of the song.
4. SpacesAdds hype finish beats inside turns—triggering GO! (Space) or YEAH! (Ins / 0) calls to cap off phrases.

02 · Call & Response Sync

Golden rule: the Player Turn must mirror the NPC Turn 1:1.

One-Two Party is the strictest mode on phrase symmetry. When an NPC calls a rhythm, the player's response must match it beat for beat.

  1. 01

    Pair every NPC Change with a Player Change

    Place a Change marker before the NPC calls their sequence, and another Change marker immediately before the player's turn begins (typically 1, 2, or 4 measures apart depending on phrase length).

  2. 02

    Match note timing identically

    If the NPC hits beats at 0, 4, 8, 10, 12 inside their measure, the Player Turn immediately below must place Notes at the exact same relative offsets (0, 4, 8, 10, 12) plus any matching Space cue.

  3. 03

    Keep turns at 11 notes or fewer

    Standard One-Two Party moves scale cleanly from 1 to 11 notes per turn. Exceeding 11 notes inside a single Change segment overflows standard move choreography and triggers validator warnings.

  4. 04

    Use Dance Breaks to pace energy

    Insert Dance Break markers between sections (verse → chorus, chorus → bridge) to transition back into dancing and give players breathing room before faster rounds. Mark the last major pose at the end as your Final Pose.

Common Mistake: Unsynced NPC & Player Beats

Never add an extra note or move a Spacebeat in the Player Turn without updating the matching NPC Demo turn right above it. Mismatched timing causes players to hear the wrong call or miss cues unfairly.

03 · Space Cues & Difficulty

Using GO! / YEAH! cues and 4K vs. 8K keys.

Add variety cleanly by placing Space accents at phrase endings and balancing key counts across the song.

GO! and YEAH! Space Calls

  • GO! (Spacebar): Great for snappy 1-measure or 2-measure call endings right on beat 4 or the "&" before beat 4.
  • YEAH! (Insert / Numpad 0): Ideal at the peak of high-energy chorus rounds or right before a Dance Break transition.
  • Sync rule: Just like regular arrows, any Space cue placed inside the NPC Demo turn must appear at the exact same relative beat in the Player Turn.

4-Key vs. 8-Key Charting

  • 4-Key (2, 4, 6, 8): Focuses on clear rhythmic callouts using the four cardinal directions—best for fast BPM tracks and approachable difficulties.
  • 8-Key (1–9): Adds diagonal arrows for richer choreography while preserving the exact same Call-and-Response timing framework.
  • Difficulty Curve: Open at Lv 1–4 (2–4 notes per phrase), ramp choruses to Lv 6–9, and save dense Lv 10–11 phrases for peak drops.

04 · Final Check

Pre-test One-Two Party checklist.

Verify these items in live preview before finishing your chart.

  • Every Player Turn mirrors its paired NPC Turn 1:1 on both Notes and Space cues.
  • No single Change segment exceeds 11 notes.
  • NPC and Player Change boundaries sit cleanly on matching measure/beat intervals.
  • Dance Breaks separate sections smoothly without cutting off active Player Turns.
  • A Final Pose closes out the end of the choreography cleanly.
  • Difficulty ramps progressively from early verses into the final chorus.
